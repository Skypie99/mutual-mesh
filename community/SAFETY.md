# Mutual Mesh — Community Safety

**Owner:** Casey (Community Manager).
**Status:** v1 — 2026-05-25. Review before Phase 3 / wider web demo distribution.

This document is a community promise, not a legal contract. It's written for Mara — a skeptical young parent who's been burned by apps that overpromise — and for Keo, a trans organizer who has real reasons to distrust platforms. If a sentence sounds like a terms-of-service, we'll rewrite it.

---

## What makes this a safe space

**Invite-only is protection, not exclusion.**
You can only join Mutual Mesh if someone already in the community gives you an invite code. This isn't about gatekeeping who's "worthy." It's about keeping out scrapers, harassers, and people who aren't here to share resources. If you're here, someone vouched — not by name, just by handing you a code.

**No real names, ever.**
You pick a handle when you sign up. That handle is all other people ever see. We don't collect your real name, and we have no way to connect your handle to your legal identity — not us, not admins, not anyone.

**Verification is done by humans, not algorithms.**
When you apply, a community admin — a real person embedded in your local network — looks at your account and makes a human call. This usually takes about 24 hours. There's no automated approval score. There's no shadowban. If you're rejected, we'll tell you why.

**What admins can see — and what they can't.**
During verification, an admin sees: your chosen handle, your email, your postal prefix (the first three characters — neighborhood-level, not your street), and whether your invite code was valid. That's it.

Admins cannot see: your address, your phone number, your real name, any prior posts or claims (you haven't made any yet), IP address, or device info. After they decide, they don't retain access to your data. A second admin reviewing your account sees only the current state — not who reviewed before them or what they thought.

Admins are regular members with a bit of extra responsibility. They are not moderators with power over your content, and they cannot see more of your data than any other verified user once you're approved.

## Posting guidelines

Keep it brief: this isn't a rulebook, it's a short list.

- **Post real needs and real offers.** If you're offering a can of formula, it should be in your hand. If you need it, say so plainly.
- **No commercial listings.** This isn't OfferUp or Facebook Marketplace. If you're selling something, this isn't the place.
- **No requests for cash, e-transfers, or gift cards.** Full stop.
- **No listing items you don't actually have.** If something gets claimed before you can hand it off, mark it done — don't leave it up.

If a listing looks wrong to you, long-press it to report it. We'll take a look.

## Receiving resources safely

**You never have to share your address publicly.**
When you claim a resource, Mutual Mesh shows you the poster's contact handle — a Signal number, an email alias, a Telegram handle, whatever they chose. You reach out to them on that channel and arrange the handoff between the two of you. Your address doesn't appear anywhere in the app.

**For pickups from someone you don't know personally:**
- Meet in a public place for a first exchange — a library, a Tim Hortons, the lobby of a community center.
- Bring a friend if you want to.
- Trust your instincts. If something feels off, you don't have to go through with it. No one here will judge you.

**No-shows are okay.**
Life is hard and complicated. If you can't make a handoff happen — or if someone doesn't show for yours — that's not a character flaw. We don't keep scores. We don't issue warnings for no-shows. Just update the listing if the item is still available.

## If something goes wrong

**Flagging a bad listing:**
Long-press any listing → tap "Report." Or email us at privacy@mutualmesh.ca with the listing handle and a brief description of what you saw.

**Flagging unsafe behaviour:**
If someone made you feel unsafe — through messages, in person, or in the app — email privacy@mutualmesh.ca. Be as detailed or as brief as you're comfortable being.

**What happens after you report:**
A human reads every report. No automated filters, no algorithmic strike systems. We'll contact the person who reported (if you want us to — you can report anonymously too) and take action based on what we find. For serious safety concerns, we'll act quickly. For policy questions, we'll aim to respond within 48 hours.

Depending on severity, outcomes range from a note to the poster, to removal of the listing, to account suspension, to a permanent ban. We don't share what action we took — that's between us and the reported user. But we do take action.

We don't have a "three strikes" algorithm. Every report is a human judgment call.

## For admins

You're a community member with an extra 20 minutes of work a day. That's the whole job description.

**Verification is a responsibility, not a gatekeeping power.**
Your job is to apply the criteria (see `community/onboarding.md`) and let people in. It's not to protect the community from people who make you uncomfortable or who you don't personally know. If you notice yourself looking for reasons to reject rather than reasons to approve, that's a signal to flag for your coordinator.

**What you see, and only what you see:**
During verification, you have access to a verified applicant's handle, email, postal prefix, and invite-code status. After you decide, your session ends and you retain no ongoing access to that account's data. You cannot view resource posts, claim history, or contact handles for users you've approved — not through the admin interface, and not through the database (RLS enforces this at the technical layer).

**If you discover an access gap — a way to see data you shouldn't be able to see — treat it as a security report**, not a privilege. File it at privacy@mutualmesh.ca and we'll fix it fast.

**Escalate before you decide alone.**
If an application is contested, unusual, or you're just not sure, escalate. That's what the process is for. Slow and careful is better than fast and wrong.
