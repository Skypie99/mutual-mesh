## What's NOT a friction (deliberately)

These were flagged during persona writing but are intentional design constraints, not bugs:

- **Slow verification (24h)** — Mara and Keo both flagged this. But: it's the load-bearing trust mechanism. Faster verification would mean lower-quality vetting. Riley confirms: keep at ~24h.
- **No real-name identity** — would create friction at signup. But it's the load-bearing privacy choice. Confirmed by Jordan D1.
- **No in-app chat** — Mara and Deb would benefit. But it'd put Mutual Mesh in a different regulatory category and create moderation surface. v2 if ever. MVP decision confirmed.
- **Pull-only updates** — would feel slow. But notifications leak data. Confirmed by Sky's MVP scope decision.
- **No "score" or "reputation"** — Deb explicitly anti-wants this. Confirmed.

## Insights that cut across personas

### I1: Trust is the load-bearing metric, not engagement

Every persona's anti-goals were about **what would make them leave**, not what would make them stay. Standard "engagement loop" tactics (gamification, streaks, notifications) would actively destroy the product for the audience. Casey and Dani must reject any growth-team instinct toward these patterns.

### I2: The product is a coordination tool, not a community

Users coordinate via Mutual Mesh and trust via Signal / Proton / their existing relationships. Mutual Mesh is the matching layer; the trust layer is upstream. Don't try to be a social network.

### I3: The audience is structurally diverse — never collapse to a monolith

Mara (parent, urban, Android), Keo (trans, harm-reduction, iOS), Deb (organizer, stable-housing, iOS): these three personas use Mutual Mesh for entirely different reasons. UI / copy / categories must accommodate breadth. Beware any feature that implicitly assumes one of the three archetypes is the "main" user.

### I4: Casey's seed-drive strategy is the load-bearing growth lever

A Mutual Mesh with 3 listings is worse than no Mutual Mesh — it sets users up for disappointment. Casey's plan to seed one neighborhood at a time, with real partners delivering real listings before public invites, is the difference between a working product and a dead one.

### I5: Most of the "v2" features identified are schema deltas

Bulk-post, auto-expire, push notifications, categories, "I picked it up" confirmation — all of these require schema changes Dana would need to apply. Phase 0b lands the v1 schema; v2 schema becomes its own decision point. Plan for this.
